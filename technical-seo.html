Technical SEO / Audit & recommendations

Three technical SEO issues I identified on a listings site

I compared source HTML with rendered pages, investigated listing URL patterns and traced what happened after a motorcycle sold. I worked with developers on all three issues. Together we resolved most of the problems, and I defined the rule for retaining sold listings.

1. Listing pages depended on JavaScript

Some cards remain visible with JavaScript disabled, while filters and parts of the layout change. I recommended making important content and links available in the initial HTML.

2. Listing URLs needed a readable structure

Legacy pattern

/ad-details/{uuid}
The identifier gave buyers little context about the motorcycle. I documented the pattern and the migration risks.

Updated pattern

/oglas/{slug}
The updated structure uses a readable listing slug. I worked with developers on the change and flagged legacy redirects for verification.

3. What happens to a listing after a sale?

The search problem

Some listings had already earned positions in Google. When a motorcycle sold, its page disappeared and the marketplace lost that search presence.

The rule I defined

Keep the sold listing available for up to 45 days, then return HTTP 404. That gives the team a consistent retirement policy and avoids removing a ranking page the moment an item sells.
